
# Octo: An Open-Source Generalist Robot Policy


## The five fields

- Action representation: Continuous action chunks generated by a small
diffusion head conditioned on the transformer's output. Predicts
multiple future timesteps per forward pass (action chunking, typically
4 actions per chunk). No discretization, no binning. K denoising
steps (~5-10) per chunk.
- Backbone: Small transformer trained from scratch on Open-X data
(~27M and ~93M param variants). NOT a pretrained VLM. Modular
tokenization: each input modality (cameras, robot state, language
instruction, goal image) has its own small encoder producing tokens
that get concatenated into a single sequence the transformer attends
over. "Readout tokens" aggregate information for the diffusion head.
- Data mixture: Subset of Open-X Embodiment, ~800k trajectories,
curated and balanced across the 25 sub-datasets used. Pure robot
data, no web co-training (relies on Open-X diversity for breadth).
- Control freq / latency: Higher than RT-2/OpenVLA due to (a) small
backbone, (b) action chunking amortizing forward passes, (c) cheap
diffusion head. Effective control rates of tens of Hz are achievable.
- Central claim: A flexible transformer policy with modular per-modality
tokenization and a continuous diffusion action head can serve as a
generalist starting point that fine-tunes cheaply and effectively to
new robots, sensor suites, and action spaces, outperforming prior
generalists on flexibility, precision, and speed.

## What's new vs. OpenVLA / RT-2

- Drops discrete action tokenization entirely. Replaces with a continuous
diffusion head over action chunks.
- Drops the big pretrained VLM backbone. Uses a small transformer trained
from scratch on robot data instead, betting that action-head sophistication
matters more than backbone scale.
- Modular per-modality tokenization makes the architecture polymorphic
across observation and action spaces, no surgery required to add
sensors or change action dimensionality.
- Action chunking: predicts N future actions in one forward pass,
amortizing inference cost over multiple control steps.
- Architecture is _designed_ for fine-tuning to new robots, not just
fine-tuning to new tasks.

## Key design choice + why


The diffusion head over continuous action chunks. This single choice
buys: (a) precision (no quantization error), (b) speed (one forward
pass per chunk, not per token), (c) multi-modal action distributions
(can place mass on multiple valid grasps), and (d) compatibility with
flexible action dimensionalities (just resize the head's output). It's
the architectural decision that defines Octo's place in the lineage and
the one that influences nearly every VLA after it.


## Most important findings

1. Continuous diffusion-head policies match or beat discrete-token
policies on standard manipulation benchmarks at substantially higher
inference speeds.
2. Modular tokenization enables fine-tuning to new robots with new
sensor combinations using small datasets (often <100 episodes).
3. Action chunking is empirically important, predicting and executing
chunks of multiple actions outperforms single-step prediction at
matched compute, partly due to higher effective control rates and
partly due to smoother action sequences.
4. A small from-scratch transformer can rival much larger VLM-backed
policies for many manipulation tasks, but loses on tasks requiring
open-vocabulary semantic generalization.

## Limitations

- No inherited web knowledge. Without a pretrained VLM backbone, Octo's
language understanding and open-vocabulary visual recognition lag
OpenVLA significantly. "Pick up the extinct animal"-style emergent
capabilities are out of reach.
- Diffusion at inference still costs K denoising steps. Faster than
autoregressive token decoding, but not as fast as flow matching
(which π0 will demonstrate).
- The from-scratch backbone caps the model's reasoning ability. There's
no path to chain-of-thought reasoning or instruction-following beyond
what robot data teaches.
- Octo is generalist-shaped but still underperforms task-specific
fine-tuned models in many narrow settings, true of every generalist,
but worth noting.
